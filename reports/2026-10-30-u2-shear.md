# MOOD U2 on shear layers: which test rejects, and whether a curvature-scaled DMP tolerance helps (2026-10-30)

Follow-up to `2026-10-30-benchmarks2d.md` §2 (U2 drops 2–9 % of the particles per step in the Kelvin–Helmholtz shear
layers and dissipates the Gresho vortex). Question: does the proposed fix work, i.e. accept DMP violations up to
`C h² max|c|` and keep Diot's ratio test as the shock detector, and does MOOD still trigger where it has to?

**Short answer: no.** The proposal rests on two assumptions that the data contradict:
1. *the ratio test separates jumps from smooth layers*: it fails for 92–98 % of the shear-layer violations as well;
2. *the shear-layer violations are O(h² u'') errors of a resolved smooth profile*: their statistics do not change
   with resolution (128² … 512²) or on a lattice; they are grid-scale oscillations, which MOOD is designed to flag.

A tolerance in curvature units without the ratio test does recover Kelvin–Helmholtz, Gresho and the vortex, but it
also lets shock oscillations grow (C4/C12 min ρ 20 % below the initial minimum, Sod undershoot 1e-2): at a
discontinuity the oscillation raises its own curvature scale, so the tolerance is self-reinforcing. Two safer
variants (a two-sided curvature scale, a direction-aware sign test) keep the bounds but do not help. Nothing is
proposed for merging; the HyperCloudCore experiments are on branch `claude/mood-u2-shear` (not a PR).

Tools (this branch, `tools/mood_u2_shear/`): `diag_u2.jl` (per-test logging: for every DMP violation of the full-order
forward-Euler candidate at NSNAP times, the violation `v`, local range `R`, global range `G`, curvature scales `τ1`
from gradient differences (`h² max_j |(∇u_j − ∇u_i)·x|/|x|²`, nearest neighbours) and `τ2 = h² max|c|`, sign/ratio
outcomes, plateau, interface mismatch), `u2diag_analyse.jl` and `comp_analyse.jl` (quantiles, per component),
`e2e_u2.jl` (end-to-end metrics and MOOD activity; the keys `MOOD_smooth` / `MOOD_directional` exist only on the
experimental HyperCloudCore branch). All runs: CPU, Float64, jitter 0.2 unless noted, AlignedRusanov, frozen MLS 2.

## 1. Which test rejects (U2 as on main, HyperCloudCore 0.7.9)

Non-plateau violations, K = 3 (EPD2, MLS2):

| case | violating particles | sign test fails | ratio test fails | both |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Kelvin–Helmholtz 128² (t ≤ 0.25) | 2.8–3.4 % | 84 % | 95 % | 82 % |
| Gresho 100² (t ≤ 0.4) | 3.6–5.4 % | 92 % | 98 % | 92 % |
| Implosion 100² | 1.4–7.3 % | 82 % | 92 % | 80 % |
| Lax–Liu C4 / C6 / C12 150² | 1.1–3.7 % | 84–87 % | 97 % | 83–86 % |

Every test the rejected particles could pass fails at a shear layer as often as at a shock. Dropping the sign test
and keeping the ratio test would accept at most the ~5 % of the shear-layer violations where the ratio test passes.

**(b) Two-sided scale** (a curvature counts only if neighbours on opposite sides see it; meant to close the foot of a
jump): KH K=3 1.4e-2 / 7.7e-3 / 7.8e-3 for C = 0.25 / 0.5 / 1, Gresho 0.185 / 0.100 / 0.041, but C4 still
[0.402, 2.45] and Sod (EPD1, MLS3) still undershoots 1.1e-2. Inside a smeared 2-D shock both sides carry curvature.

**(c) Direction-aware sign test** (accept a smooth extremum along one direction when the others are monotone, e.g.
ρv of the seed mode across the tanh layer): bounds unchanged everywhere, but no gain either (KH K=3 2.8e-4, activity
3.51 %; Gresho 0.269). The rejected violations fail in their own extremum direction, consistent with §2.

## 4. Conclusions and options

- U2's tests do what they are designed for: the shear-layer and Gresho violations are grid-scale oscillations of
  the unlimited high-order update, statistically the same as those next to the 2-D shocks. A criterion on these
  local features cannot accept one and reject the other; any tolerance proportional to a local curvature or range
  is self-reinforcing at discontinuities.
- What makes MOOD expensive in accuracy here is the fallback, not the detection: a flagged particle goes to order 1
  (and with EPD2 its neighbourhood too). The benchmark tables already show it: EPD1 keeps the KH mode near 0.01,
  EPD2 lets it decay to 1e-4; VK, a smooth a priori limiter, reaches 0.196 (KH) and 0.021 (Gresho).
- Options, in the order I would try them:
  1. **A limited second-order fallback** (MOOD drops to a VK/BJ-limited MUSCL2 before order 1, as in Diot's and
     Clain's cascades): keeps the detection, so the shock bounds stay, and makes a spurious drop cost a limited
     second-order update instead of first order. Library work in HyperCloudCore (MUSCL with a limiter used only below
     the full order) plus a run of the benchmark suite.
  2. For smooth or shear-dominated benchmarks, VK with `MOODPAD` (admissibility only, Core PR #26).
  3. EPD1 instead of EPD2 where only accuracy matters (EPD2's forcing multiplies the drops).
- Not recommended: the curvature-scaled tolerance as a default; as an opt-in it would only be safe on problems
  without discontinuities.

MOOD activity (share of interior particles below K, averaged): KH 3.5 % (K3 EPD2), 7.8 % (EPD1), 3.6 % (+VK
fallback), 0 % (VK+PAD); the share at order 1 does not change with the fallback (3.0–3.1 % in KH, 4.6 % in Gresho).
Wall time: VK+PAD runs 2–4× faster than the MOOD methods (e.g. Gresho 10–17 s against 23 s, implosion 13–19 s
against 33–69 s).

- **Option 1 (limited fallback): no effect worth having.** Within ±60 % of a bad KH result (3.1e-4 … 6.0e-4 against
  VK 6.0e-3), Gresho and the shocks unchanged. The candidates of the flagged particles still violate the DMP with a
  limited MUSCL2 reconstruction, so the cascade still ends at order 1 for the same particles. Not proposed.
- **Option 3 (EPD1 instead of EPD2): 2–4× more accurate on smooth flows, slightly looser bounds.** Twice as many
  particles drop, but without EPD2's forcing a drop costs less. C4/C12 keep the minimum within 0.15 % (more particles
  marginally below it), the Sod overshoot grows from 1e-4 to 2.5e-4; the C4/C12 maxima rise (also with VK, which has
  no undershoot, so this is at least partly sharper resolution, not overshoot). Still 10–20× worse than VK on KH and
  Gresho.
- **Option 2 (VK + MOODPAD): the best method on every test here, and the cheapest.** Identical to VK where VK stays
  positive; on Noh it removes VK's negative pressure (−4.6e-3 → +1.1e-6) at unchanged accuracy. At K = 3 it beats all
  MOOD variants on KH (7.1e-3), Gresho (0.035), the vortex (5.7e-3), Sod (L1 7.9e-3, no over/undershoot), Noh and the
  implosion symmetry; C4 has 43 particles marginally below the initial minimum (0.5059, 0.1 %).

Recommendation: VK + PAD (K = 2 or 3) as the default for the benchmark suite and for smooth/shear problems; keep the
U2 MOOD methods for the cases where a strict DMP matters, preferably with EPD1. Proposed for `methods_review.jl` (not
done): a variant `VKPAD` (limiter VK `:hard`, MOOD criterion `:PAD`, strategy EPD1) for K = 2 … 4. No HyperCloudCore
change needed; the fallback branch stays unmerged.
